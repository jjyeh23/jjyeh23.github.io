Hello! I am a Master’s student in the School of Environmental and Forest Sciences (SEFS) and the Astrobiology Program (UWAB) at the University of Washington. I am combining plant ecology and astrobiology in the [Remote Sensing and Geospatial Analysis Laboratory](https://sites.uw.edu/rsgal/), studying dynamic landscape change with a focus on wildflower ecosystems in extreme climate conditions.

My work sits at the intersection of **environmental science**, **ecology**, and **astrobiology**.

## My Background

My primary background is in geophysics and planetary science, earning my Bachelor of Science degree at the University of Washington with a major in Earth and Space Sciences and a minor in Physics. Overall, my interdisciplinary background encourages me to explore dynamic changes both on Earth and beyond.

## Current Work

My current research focuses on **wildflower phenology under changing climate conditions**. I am investigating how climate influences the timing of vegetation processes, and how remote sensing can help us observe these phenomena through space and time.

My interests include:
- **Environmental change** and vegetation response
- **Wildflower phenology** and seasonal biological processes
- **Remote sensing** and Earth observation
- **Climate and vegetation relationships**
- **Spectral signatures of vegetation**

## Astrobiology & Planetary Environments

Alongside environmental science, I am also pursuing **astrobiology** as a developing direction of my research, exploring how Earth-based and remotely-sensed ecological processes might translate to planetary environments. In particular, I am interested in understanding how life interacts with changing environments and what that might look like elsewhere.
